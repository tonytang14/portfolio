Measurement and Instrumentation

Overview

For my Measurement and Instrumentation class, I investigated how bench press grip width affects muscle activation in the chest, shoulders, and triceps. Because greater muscle activation is generally associated with increased muscular engagement and potential strength development, I wanted to determine whether changing grip width could be used to target specific muscle groups. For the experiment, I used non-invasive surface EMG sensors placed on the skin to measure muscle activity. Logger Pro was used as the data acquisition system, and MATLAB was used to process and analyze the collected data. The results showed that increasing the grip width from 15 cm to 85 cm produced a 133% increase in chest muscle activation, while shoulder and triceps activation decreased by 25% and 73%, respectively. These findings suggest that a wider grip places greater emphasis on the chest, while a narrower grip increases the relative involvement of the shoulders and triceps.
